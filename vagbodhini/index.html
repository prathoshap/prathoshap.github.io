What it is

Not transcription — a practice companion.

Vāgbodhinī helps you learn to chant Sanskrit correctly. You paste a śloka in any script, it splits the verse into pādas by metre and synthesises the correct chant with our metre-aware TTS (Vāgdhenu). You chant along, and a Sanskrit speech-recognition model scores each syllable — showing what landed and what to work on. Because the target text is known, the tool verifies your recitation rather than guessing it, so it stays reliable even where open transcription is hard.

How it works

1

Paste a śloka — any script

Devanāgarī, Kannada, Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam, Grantha, or Roman (IAST/HK/ITRANS). The script is auto-detected and echoed back so you can confirm.

2

Hear the reference chant

The metre (vṛtta) is detected and the verse is split into pāda (¼) · ardha (½) · full. Each is chanted by Vāgdhenu — learn quarter by quarter, then the whole verse.

3

Chant, and get per-syllable feedback

Record your recitation; every akshara is marked correct / to-fix / unclear, with a match score. Choose Be-strict or Be-liberal. Play your chant against the reference, and overrule the tool when you know you're right.

Under the hood

  • Vāgdhenu — a vṛtta-aware śloka-to-chant TTS renders the metre-conditioned reference.
  • Su-śrotā ASR — an IndicConformer-CTC finetuned for śāstric/recitational Sanskrit (~6% CER on clean chant), released on Hugging Face.
  • Robust scoring — akshara-level verification with decode-consensus: the tool abstains ("unclear") rather than confidently mis-flag a syllable it heard correctly.
  • Open — code and the full experiment report are on GitHub.
